The meeting room shares a screen through `/media` display capture, so it can share one panel of the page as the call does. `LiveKitRoomController` asks the browser's picker through `RequestDisplayCapture` (a constructor option, `RequestScreenShare`, lets a test pass its own), caps the shared track at 1080p and 30 fps with `applyConstraints`, as LiveKit caps its own capture, and publishes it with `publishTrack(track, { source: ScreenShare })`; stopping unpublishes it and releases the capture, and leaving releases it too. A share stopped, or ended in the browser, while the picker was open or the track was publishing is withdrawn. A picker the user closes shares nothing and is no longer reported as an error; a share the browser refuses is a `device` error with the picker's reason. `ILiveKitRoomController.SetScreenShareEnabled` and `ChangeScreenShare` take a surface as before or the capture's options (`DisplayCaptureSurface | DisplayCaptureOptions`), so one panel can be shared with its name, and `LiveKitLocalMediaState.ScreenSharePanelLabel` carries the name while it is shared; the preview room takes the same request. `ToScreenShareCaptureOptions` is deprecated. In `ng-livekit-room`, the control bar's Share menu lists panels under "This panel" (`SharePanels`, `PanelShareRequested`); `mj-livekit-room` marks its whiteboard as a panel (`mjSharePanel="Whiteboard"`), lists the panels on screen that do not hold the room, shares the picked one, names it in the share preview ("Sharing a panel: Whiteboard"), and stops the share when the shared panel goes away. The room's whiteboard shows its board again: its surface set `display: block` on the board host, which overrode the host's flex column and left the board with no height.
