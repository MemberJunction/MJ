A realtime model's video output comes as typed frames. Core adds `RealtimeVideoFrame`, a union of three kinds, each with its MIME type: `fmp4` (a piece of fragmented MP4, `Piece: 'init' | 'fragment'`), `chunk` (one encoded frame, H.264 in Annex B form or VP8, VP9 or AV1, with a required `PresentationTimeMs` and `KeyFrame`) and `image` (JPEG, PNG, WebP). `PresentationTimeMs` is on the stream's own media timeline, not wall-clock time; `Width` and `Height` are optional. `Fmp4PieceToVideoFrame` builds an fMP4 frame from a model part: the piece from its first box, an init's picture size from its video track, and a fragment's time and key-frame flag from its first video sample; bytes neither labelled `video/mp4` nor opening with an MP4 box give `null`. `IRealtimeSession.OnVideoFrame` replaces `OnAvatarOutput` and `RealtimeAvatarMediaChunk`, which were added earlier in this release, and `OnVideoOutput` is deprecated (hosts still forward it; it goes in the next major version). A granted bridged Gemini avatar, on the Developer API or Gemini Enterprise, sends its pieces as frames through `OnVideoFrame`, and the browser Gemini client hands frames to `VideoPlayout.Append(frame)` (`IAvatarVideoPlayout.Append` takes a frame); both drop a `video/*` part that isn't MP4 and report it once per type. The bridge engine forwards each frame on `video-out` with its MIME type and copies what the frame says about its video onto the bridge frame (`Width`, `Height`, `KeyFrame`: an image is a key frame, an fMP4 init carries its own size, and an fMP4 fragment with video carries its key-frame flag and the size of the stream's init); in a full-duplex multi-agent room the floor gate reads an fMP4 frame's piece (an init passes, a fragment counts its audio), and encoded chunks and images pass ungated because their voice comes as PCM, which is gated. `VideoPlayout` plays `fmp4` frames and drops any other kind, reporting `'no-decoder'` once.
