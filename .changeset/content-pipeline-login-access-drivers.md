Two login Access drivers open a cookie session for a members-only site, with the user name and password from MJ: Credentials (mapped by `Configuration.AccessCredentials`, as for `Credential`) and the rest from the source's settings.

`FormLogin` posts the login form through `network-utils`' `HttpRequest` with the SSRF guard on every hop (`HttpResponse.SetCookies` keeps each `Set-Cookie` header on its own; `SafeFetch` / `HttpRequest` gain an `OnRedirect` hook that sees each redirect and may change the next request), keeping cookies in a `tough-cookie` jar (RFC 6265 matching, public suffixes refused) (optionally after a GET of the login page for its cookies and hidden fields, as a form or JSON, with `LoginUsernameField` / `LoginPasswordField` / `LoginExtraFields`, and `LoginSuccessMarker` / `LoginFailureMarker`), following redirects through the SSRF guard and keeping their cookies. `BrowserLogin` drives the login page in the Headless renderer's browser (pre-click, user name, continue, password, submit, confirm, success selector, cookie marker). Both return `Headers.Cookie` for `SessionOrigins` (default the source's origin), expiring with the first cookie or `SessionTtlMinutes`. `BrowserLogin` checks every redirect hop against the SSRF guard (a redirected navigation becomes a checked navigation; a posted login redirected with 307/308 is followed only to its own origin, so the credentials are never re-posted to another site; other requests' redirects are followed hop by hop) and refuses WebSockets, which request routing does not see. It also puts the browser's storage state, cut to the session's origins (their cookies and `localStorage`; an identity provider's own cookies stay behind), in `AccessArtifact.Extensions.StorageState` (`SessionStorageState` in content-pipeline-base), and the `Headless` renderer restores it, cookies and localStorage, into each page on the session's origins. The server-bootstrap class manifest registers both drivers.
