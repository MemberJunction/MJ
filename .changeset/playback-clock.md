The face moves with the voice: `/media` adds `IPlaybackClock` (`CurrentTimeMs`, the media time of the audio heard now, or `null`), and `VideoPlayoutOptions.Clock` gives it to the player. `RealtimePcmPlayback` is such a clock: `IRealtimePcmPlayback.Enqueue(pcm16, mediaTimeMs?)` takes an optional media time, each chunk keeps its place on the audio context's clock (an untimed chunk continues the one before it), and `CurrentTimeMs` reads the context's time less the output latency the browser reports (`outputLatency`, `baseLatency`); `Flush` forgets it all. With a clock, the WebCodecs and image decoders show each frame when the voice reaches its `PresentationTimeMs`, the meeting bot's rules on media time: a frame more than 500 ms behind the voice is dropped, one ahead of it waits, and one more than 2 s ahead is dropped; a frame that starts a new stream waits while the voice still plays the old one. When the voice stops, frames carry on by their own times from where it was; before it starts, they wait up to 500 ms, then go by their own times. MSE ignores the clock (an MP4 that carries the voice keeps the two together), and Gemini passes none: its PCM carries no media time. Drivers that time nothing behave as before.
