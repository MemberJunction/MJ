A call's Share menu offers "This panel ▸", listing the panels of the page that are on screen: the call's Whiteboard and Explorer's main content ("Main content"). Picking one shares that element alone. A new `[mjSharePanel]="<label>"` directive (with `mjSharePanelIcon`) in `ng-realtime-media` marks a shareable region and registers it with the app's `SharePanelRegistry`, whose `PanelsFor$(host)` lists the panels whose box overlaps the viewport, in page order, leaving out any panel that holds the call itself, and nothing where the browser cannot share a single panel (`Supported`: `getDisplayMedia` plus Element or Region Capture, so Chrome and Edge on the desktop). Registry changes reach its streams in a microtask. A channel opts its call surface in with `BaseRealtimeChannelClient.SurfaceShareable` (default `false`; the Whiteboard channel returns `true`), and the Explorer shell marks `mj-tab-container#mj-main-content`, so `ng-explorer-core` now depends on `ng-realtime-media`. The call overlay passes the list to the composer's Share menus (`RealtimeComposerComponent.SharePanels`), starts `StartScreenShare({ Panel, PanelLabel })` for a pick, and stops the share when the shared panel goes away; a panel only out of sight keeps its share. In `/media`, a panel share now uses Element Capture wherever the browser has it (Chromium 132+), giving the panel `isolation: isolate` inline for the share and its own value back when the share ends, and Region Capture otherwise; `DisplayCaptureOptions.PanelLabel` names the panel and `DisplayCapture.PanelLabel` carries the name. The runtime's share state carries it (`RealtimeCaptureState.PanelLabel`), the agent sees the share as "Whiteboard (shared panel)" in the "Agent can see" chip and its switch note, and `mj-share-preview` reads "Sharing a panel: Whiteboard" (`SharePreviewComponent.PanelLabel`). The Screen Share channel's state still reports `surface: 'tab'`.
