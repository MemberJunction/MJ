The meeting bot publishes an agent's avatar. Before an agent's meeting session opens, the room coordinator asks the native room module whether the bot can publish one (`describeAvatarVideo`: `@livekit/rtc-node` with video publishing, and ffmpeg 4.2 or later with the H.264 and AAC decoders, from `MJ_FFMPEG_PATH` or the `PATH`, probed once per thread); only then does the session ask for it (`RealtimeAvatarSettings.Delivery: 'room'`, threaded through the bridged session prep), and the Gemini driver grants it on an endpoint that renders avatars (VIDEO, the avatar at 2 Mbps), with the decision on `IRealtimeSession.AvatarStatus`. A granted bridged Gemini session sends the avatar's fragmented MP4 as typed frames through `IRealtimeSession.OnVideoFrame`, plays PCM only before a turn's first video, drops media from `interrupted` to that turn's `turnComplete`, and reports the avatar video it generated as `OutputTokenDetails.VideoSeconds` deltas through `OnUsage` (counted up to `generationComplete`). The engine sends the pieces on `video-out` with their MIME type; in a full-duplex multi-agent room each piece passes the floor gate by its audio's duration (`FullDuplexTurnGate.OnOutputDuration`). The LiveKit bridge routes `video/mp4` frames to the room client (`publishAvatarMedia`), which reads them with Core's fMP4 reader (`ReadFmp4Fragment`, `AvccToAnnexB`, `AdtsHeader`, and `Fmp4AudioSeconds` beside AV9's `Fmp4VideoSeconds`; `ReadFmp4Init` also reads a track's size, the trex sample size and flags, the H.264 parameter sets and the AAC config), decodes them in two long-lived ffmpeg child processes, queues the voice on the bot's audio track and shows each frame on a camera track (`agent-avatar`, LiveKit's default simulcast, published at the first frame) when the voice's playout reaches the frame's timestamp. A barge-in drops the queued frames and fences both decoders; the last frame stays on screen. A decoder that dies is restarted; three failures of one decoder within 60 s, or a refused camera track, take the avatar down: the track is unpublished, the bot sets `mj.agentAvatar` to `audio-only:<reason>` (agent bot tokens now carry `canUpdateOwnMetadata`), and the engine replaces the model session with an audio-only one (`StartBridgeSessionParams.RecoverRealtimeSessionWithoutAvatar`). The bot's join token carries `mj.agentAvatar` (`on`, or `audio-only:<reason>` with `decoder-missing` when the host has no usable ffmpeg); Core adds the attribute helpers and the reasons `decoder-missing`, `decoder-failed` and `publish-failed`, which `ParseRealtimeAvatarStatus` reads and `REALTIME_AVATAR_UNAVAILABLE_REASONS` lists (from the same table); the call and mobile notices word them "Audio only for {Agent}: the avatar couldn't be shown in this meeting". `GeminiEnterpriseRealtime` renders a bridged session's avatar the same way. With the media worker on, the pieces are transferred to the worker, a restarted worker gets the stream's init again, and one restarted after a take-down joins audio only. Integration check RD17 (the LiveKit provider allows video out, and the native module answers the avatar probe). The MJAPI image installs a pinned LGPL static ffmpeg (BtbN) checked against its sha256; the pin values must be filled in before the image builds (or build with `INSTALL_FFMPEG=false`).
