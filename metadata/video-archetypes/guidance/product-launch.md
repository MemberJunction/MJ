# Product Launch Guidance

## Purpose
Use the Product Launch archetype for crisp, high-energy 15 to 30 second films introducing a new capability, release, or workflow across widescreen and vertical formats. Every frame should center on the real product experience and the concrete friction it removes.

## Beat Intent
- **hook**: Land the core capability or outcome in the first two seconds with kinetic typography and an authentic glimpse of the interface in action.
- **friction**: Contrast the old slow or fragmented workflow in a single concise beat so the viewer immediately recognizes the pain point.
- **reveal**: Transition cleanly into the new product experience, highlighting the exact control, command, or screen that changes the workflow.
- **proof**: Walk through two or three rapid, legible UI state transitions showing the feature completing a real task.
- **close**: End on the product name, release availability, and a clear next action with generous hold time on the final card.

## What to Avoid
- Never use generic abstract 3D shapes or stock footage in place of the actual product interface.
- Avoid tiny, uncropped full-desktop screenshots on 9:16 vertical compositions; re-frame around the active control and callout.
- Do not let transitions obscure UI text before the viewer can read the active state.

## Facts Rule
Never invent product screens, UI controls, customer logos, performance numbers, or people. Product UI must come only from supplied or deterministically captured screenshots in `Assets.Approved`, and any performance claim must bind to `DataBindings`. If screenshots or metrics are missing, ask the caller instead of generating fake UI.
